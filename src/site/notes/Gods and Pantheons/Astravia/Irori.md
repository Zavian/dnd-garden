The **Master of Masters** and the final pillar of the **Nyphron Covenant**. Irori represents self-perfection, discipline, and the belief that humanity can transcend its limitations not through magic or divine intervention, but through sheer will and physical mastery.

## The Path to Perfection

The followers of Irori claim that he was once a mortal man from the distant east of Astravia who achieved absolute physical and mental perfection, and thus attained divinity. While many in [[Locales/Astravia/Avryn/Avryn\|Avryn]] are wary of his strict adherents, the disciplined regimen of the Master of Masters is gaining popularity among the martial scholars of [[Rhenydd\|Rhenydd]] and the ascetics of the [[Locales/Astravia/Avryn/Sablewood\|Sablewood]] who seek order in these troubled times.

Unlike the other gods of the Covenant who represent external structures—Maribor the legacy, Abadar the law—Irori represents the internal structure. He is the patron of monks, scholars, and sages who seek to impose order on the fundamental chaos of their own minds and bodies. This focus on internal control sometimes puts his faith at odds with the wild magic of [[Gods and Pantheons/Astravia/Nethys\|Nethys]], as Irori’s followers often view reliance on raw, chaotic magic as a crutch that hinders true enlightenment.

## Appearance and Art

Irori is very rarely depicted in art because his faithful believe that any icon of him cannot hope to live up to his perfect image. Instead, they describe him as a flawless man, with no hair save a long braid, wearing simple robes and wooden sandals. To capture his likeness in stone or paint is seen as an insult to the fluidity and perfection of his true form.

## The Temples and Clergy

Temples dedicated to Irori are usually sprawling complexes featuring rooms for prayer, sleep, and exercise. Here, his faithful study and train night and day in an endless quest to achieve perfection and purify their *ki*, or life force. These temples function less like churches and more like academies or dojos, and they are not generally open to the public, reserved instead for those who have committed to the path.

Irori’s priests have no formal garb other than a long rope of braided hair tied in a loop and worn about the neck like a necklace. They teach tolerance and respect for other deities but harbor a deep disdain for those who tear down or corrupt the accomplishments of others. This has led to a quiet, ongoing feud with the followers of [[Gods and Pantheons/Astravia/Asmodeus\|Asmodeus]], whose shortcuts and devilish pacts are seen as the antithesis of Irori’s hard-earned power.

## Divine Will

Those who rise to the rank of master are said to go to Irori’s side when they die to serve him forever in the Hallows Above, while those who fail are reincarnated to begin the journey anew. Irori shows his displeasure not through grand catastrophes, but through personal failures: mysterious and sudden cramps, fatigue, or sudden setbacks in the form of unexpected illness or madness that force the devotee to restart their training. His holy text is *Unbinding the Fetters*, a lengthy tome describing physical exercises, meditation, diet, and other methods to transcend the limitations of the mortal form.
